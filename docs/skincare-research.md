# Skin tab: research notes (checked Oct 3, 2026)

Glowtone gives general skincare guidance only. **It is not medical advice.** The app tells users to see a dermatologist for serious, painful or persistent concerns.

## Skin type and concern flags (`js/skin.js`, `skinResult`)
- **Type** comes from how skin feels after cleansing and at midday, plus flakiness, dewiness, where oil shows up, and pore visibility. Scoring: dry points for tightness, flakes, dullness and midday tightness. Oil points for shine at wash time or midday, oil all over, and visible pores. A T-zone pattern means **combination**. This mirrors the self-test dermatologists describe (AAD), where you wash, wait about 30 minutes, then look at shine and tightness.
- **Flags:** *sensitive* (stinging, burning or flushing), *acne-prone* (breakouts most weeks), *dark spots / hyperpigmentation* (spots, post-acne marks, uneven tone), *dehydrated* (oily but tight, or tight without being a dry type). Dehydration means low water, not low oil, so it can happen with any type.

## Routine structure (K-beauty "layering")
In order: oil cleanser (PM) → water cleanser → toner (hydrating, patted in) → essence/serum → moisturizer → sunscreen (AM). Korean sunscreen labels pair SPF with a **PA** UVA grade (PA+ to PA++++), regulated by Korea's MFDS as a functional-cosmetic claim. The app recommends SPF 30+ / PA+++ for everyday wear and SPF 50+ / PA++++ when dark spots are flagged.

## Ingredients the app suggests (and why)
| Ingredient | Used for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Centella asiatica (cica, madecassoside) | sensitive, calming | Triterpenes support repair and calm inflammation (Bylka 2013) |
| Niacinamide | oil, pores, uneven tone, barrier | Well tolerated; sebostatic and lightening effects (Wohlrab & Kreft 2014) |
| BHA (salicylic acid) | acne, oily or congested skin | Oil-soluble exfoliant. 2–3×/week to start (AAD acne tips) |
| PHA / AHA | dullness, spots | PHA is the gentler option for sensitive skin |
| Ceramides, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, panthenol, squalane | barrier, dehydration, dry skin | Dermatologists recommend gentle, fragrance-free moisturizers (AAD dry skin) |
| Vitamin C, tranexamic acid, azelaic acid, licorice | dark spots | Daily sunscreen matters most for spots and melasma (AAD melasma) |
| Retinal / retinol | 25+, not sensitive | PM only, start 2 nights a week. Not suggested for under-18s unless a dermatologist advises |

**Be careful with:** strong actives on the same night (retinoid + AHA/BHA, or vitamin C + acids), daily exfoliation, gritty scrubs, harsh sulfates (SLS), lots of denatured alcohol, and fragrance or essential oils if sensitive. For acne-prone skin, avoid heavy occlusives on breakout areas and look for non-comedogenic labels.

## Patch testing
Following the AAD: apply a quarter-sized amount to the inner arm or the bend of the elbow, twice a day for 7–10 days. Wash it off and stop if you get redness, itching or swelling. Add one new product at a time. Leave rinse-off products on for about 5 minutes.
